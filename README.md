# AIML-Research
Crime Prediction Research
This interdisciplinary research programme investigates the structural relationships between
how human beings and artificial neural networks perceive, process, and respond to external
stimuli. The study operates on the principle that both biological and artificial systems can be
understood through a unified vocabulary of predictions, prediction errors, and failure modes.
Phase 1: Crime Prediction Model
Phase 1 treats crime as a measurable behavioral response to adverse socioeconomic stimuli,
such as poverty and population density.
• Methodology: Utilizing the Chicago Crime Dataset (7M+ records), the research employs
a suite of machine learning classifiers (Linear Regression, KNN, Decision Tree, and
Naive Bayes) to identify high-risk geographic "hotspots".
• Deliverable: An automated Alert and Reporting System that generates interactive
heatmaps and triggers notifications when the probability of high risk exceeds 0.70.
Phase 2: Neural Sentiment Visualization
This phase shifts from population-level behavior to individual cognitive pathways, mapping
how events or communications propagate through affective processing systems.
• Methodology: Fine-tuning BERT and LSTM models on datasets like GoEmotions to
visualize sentiment archetypes.
• Deliverable: A directed graph model linking Stimulus Type → Sentiment Pathway →
Decision Class (prosocial, antisocial, etc.), visualized through SHAP values and attention
heatmaps.
Phase 3: Hallucination Mapping
Phase 3 examines "hallucination" as a shared failure mode where neural systems generate
outputs ungrounded in reality.
• Comparative Framework: The research contrasts biological hallucination—driven by
predictive coding failures—with AI hallucination, which stems from training data gaps
and distributional plausibility.• Deliverable: A five-dimensional similarity-difference matrix (Root Mechanism,
Phenomenal Quality, Trigger Conditions, Self-monitoring, and Interventions).
